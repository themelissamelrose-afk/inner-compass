Heart Awakening
Meditation

A guided practice to open, soften, and return home to yourself.

Self-love is one of the most powerful, life-changing gifts. But it is also one of the most elusive and challenging to fully embrace. It cannot be given. It can only be felt as you journey within.

When you become self-responsible, embody every aspect of yourself, and let go of negative self-talk, limiting beliefs, and judgment, you open the door to true self-love. This practice is about owning your truth, releasing the past, and stepping into the present as your own best friend.

Self-love cannot be given. It can only be felt as you journey within.

Recommended: daily, to gradually move from the head to the heart.

The Practice
1
Connect with your breath
Sit comfortably, close your eyes, and begin by focusing on your breath. Inhale for six counts, hold for six, exhale for six, and hold for six before your next inhale. Repeat until you feel calm and relaxed.
2
Visualise love
Once connected to this calm state, imagine someone or a group of people you deeply value and love. Let their presence fill you with warmth.
3
Soften your heart
Draw the feeling of love into your heart area. Notice if your heart feels soft or hard, and with each exhale, feel it soften a little more.
4
Expand your compassion
Return to visualising the person you love. Let the feeling grow and expand in your heart, blessing them with words of gratitude and love. If forgiveness is needed, gently say, "I forgive you," or "I understand you're doing the best you can."
5
Extend love to others
Let the heart feelings expand by inviting others into your mind's eye. Feel for them, bless them, and if necessary, forgive them.
6
Turn the love inward
Direct that deep love toward your own body, honouring your organs, muscles and spine. Extend that same energy to the parts of you that you criticise or hide, forgiving yourself and embracing every part with unconditional love. Tell yourself, "I see you, and I forgive you."
7
Reconnect with your breath
Slowly bring your focus back to your breath, affirming: "I respect you, I forgive you, I am doing my best and I love myself for that."
Consider journaling about your experience afterwards. Noting your feelings can deepen your practice and help you recognise shifts in how you feel toward yourself and others.
